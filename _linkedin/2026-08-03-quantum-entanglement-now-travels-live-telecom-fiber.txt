For years the quantum internet had one dealbreaker.

It needed its own private fiber. Dark, undisturbed, kept clear of ordinary internet traffic.

That assumption just took a hit.

A Northwestern University team sent entangled photons 24.4 kilometers across Chicago, over commercial telecom fiber that was carrying a full load of live internet traffic at the same time.

The details, from their paper in Optica Quantum:

→ Two nodes, Evanston to downtown Chicago
→ The same fiber pushing two 800 Gbps data channels, loaded to emulate a ~36 Tbps commercial link
→ Entanglement fidelity above 94 percent

The lead researcher put it well. A quantum signal next to classical ones is like an ant walking a path full of elephants.

Their move was not to clear the road. It was to give the ant its own lane. Quantum photons in the O-band, classical traffic in the C-band, precise timing to keep the two ends locked together.

To be clear about what this is not: it is entanglement distribution, not full teleportation yet, and it still needs carefully engineered conditions. A milestone, not a product.

Here is the lesson I keep coming back to.

The assumption was that a fragile new capability needs a pristine, purpose-built environment. So progress meant building that environment from scratch.

This team asked the better question. Can we make the effect tough enough to survive the environment we already have?

The most deployable innovations rarely ask the world to be rebuilt around them. They learn to run on the world as it already is.

Before you build a dedicated stack for your new capability, ask: could it just share the one already in the ground?

What in your own work are you rebuilding, when you could be sharing?
