A 27-billion-parameter AI model just started running on an iPhone.

Not a shrunk-down demo. Not a cloud model in disguise.

The real thing. On the device. No signal required.

For years the rule felt permanent: powerful AI is too big to leave the data centre. Your phone is just a window into a server farm.

On July 14, a Caltech spinout called PrismML picked that rule apart.

Here's the number that matters:

That model, stored normally, needs about 54 GB of memory. No phone has that.

PrismML got it down to 3.9 GB.

Same 27.8 billion parameters. Squeezed by more than 13x. Still keeping 90%+ of full performance. Released free and open on Hugging Face.

How? Extreme quantization: every parameter crushed down to a single bit (+1 or -1), with a clever "group-wise scaling" trick to hold the math together.

The tradeoff is honest: raw factual recall drops. But reasoning, math and coding hold up. The part that makes a model feel smart survives.

Apple is already evaluating the tech.

Here's why every innovator should sit with this:

For years the whole industry sprinted the same direction. Bigger models. More parameters. Larger data centres. Scale, scale, scale.

PrismML asked a different question.

Not "how do we build a bigger brain in the cloud?"
But "how little can we spend to store the one we already have?"

The answer was more than 10x smaller than anyone assumed was possible.

When a whole field is optimizing the same variable, the opening is usually in the variable nobody is looking at.

While everyone races to ADD, the breakthrough is often in what you can afford to REMOVE.

Where in your work is everyone chasing "bigger," when the real move is "smaller"?
